22-seat neighbourhood restaurant · 2025

A table in two taps, and a menu that reads on a phone.

Twenty-two seats, one service a night, and a website whose menu was a PDF scan. Marta was losing bookings to whoever appeared first in a maps search — usually the chain two streets away.

01

The problem

The old site had one job — get someone to book — and put four obstacles in front of it. The menu was a 4MB PDF that opened in a viewer. The booking link went to a third-party page with different branding. The address was an image. Nothing worked one-handed on a phone at 7pm, which is when every one of these decisions is actually made.

Marta had assumed people looked at a restaurant site the way she did: at a desk, unhurried. The analytics said the opposite. Eighty-one percent of sessions were mobile, and the median was under forty seconds.

02

What we did

The whole site was designed for a person standing on a pavement deciding where to eat in the next twenty minutes. Tonight's menu is the first thing under the fold, as text, updated by Marta from her phone before service. The booking widget sits in a sticky bar that never leaves.

The dishes get real writing — where the pasta shape is from, why the ragù takes six hours — which is what turns a maps result into a decision, and incidentally what makes the page findable at all.

  • Menu as structured content, editable from a phone in under a minute
  • Sticky booking bar with the third-party widget restyled to match
  • Schema.org restaurant and menu markup for rich map results
  • Opening hours that read correctly to a search engine and to a human

03

How it was built

A tiny flat-file CMS: the menu lives in a single structured file Marta edits through a stripped-back form. No dashboard, no plugins, no monthly fee, and nothing that can be broken by an update pushed at midnight.

The reservation widget is third-party and slow, so it loads only when someone reaches for it. Until then the page is 61KB and complete.

What happened

Reservations through the site tripled within two months, and the share coming from mobile went from a third to almost all of them. Marta has changed the menu 140-odd times since launch without calling me once.

I change the menu from the kitchen between prep and service. That is the whole review.Marta Fiore, owner
