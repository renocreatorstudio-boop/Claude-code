The pricing page stopped being a locked door.

Vantage had 1,100 trials a month and a pricing page that asked people to book a call. The rebuild answers the money question on the first screen — and the sales team stopped fielding questions they never wanted.

01

The problem

Vantage sold a product people could evaluate in an afternoon, behind a site that behaved like enterprise software. Pricing was a form. Every plan said “contact us”. The founders believed a conversation would win the deal — and it did, for the small share of people willing to have one.

Session recordings settled the argument. Three in five trial users hit the pricing page in their first session; most left within nine seconds of seeing the form. They were not price-sensitive. They were unwilling to be sold to before they knew the number.

02

What we did

We put real numbers on the page — three plans, a usage slider, and a plain sentence about what happens when you outgrow a tier. The call booking stayed, moved down, and reframed as a question about migration rather than a gate on the price.

The rest of the site was rebuilt around the same instinct: answer the objection where it occurs. Every feature block ends with the limitation nobody else admits. That admission is why the page reads as honest, and honest is what converts a technical buyer.

  • A pricing model the site can express in one screen, not a spreadsheet
  • Interactive usage estimate that runs entirely client-side, no account needed
  • A twelve-component design system the in-house team now ships against
  • Docs and marketing sharing one type scale, so the seam is invisible

03

How it was built

Hand-written HTML, CSS and about 9KB of JavaScript. The usage calculator is the only interactive element and it runs without a network request, so it works before the rest of the page has finished loading.

The design system ships as CSS custom properties and a dozen documented components. Vantage's two engineers have added eleven pages since launch without asking me a question — which is the actual test of a system.

04

What happened

Trial-to-paid conversion moved from 4.4% to 6.2% in the first full quarter. Sales calls fell, and the ones that remained started later in the funnel, from people who had already priced the thing themselves.

We spent two years protecting a number that was never the problem. Putting it on the page cost us nothing and won us a quarter.Erin Sandoval, co-founder
