Researcher and keynote speaker · 2025

Fifteen years of proof, finally in one place.

Nadia's work existed — in journals, on conference stages, in three podcast archives and a university profile she could not edit. Event organisers had to assemble her credibility themselves, and often gave up.

01

The problem

The people who book keynote speakers are usually not the people who understand the research. They are programme managers with a shortlist and an afternoon. They need a bio at three lengths, a photo that will survive a projector, a topic list, past stages, and a way to check availability — and they need it without emailing anyone.

Nadia had all of it. None of it was on a page she controlled.

02

What we did

The site is a speaking kit that happens to look like an editorial magazine. One page carries the argument: what she works on, why it matters now, where she has said it before, and what she can be booked to say next. Everything an organiser might need to paste into an internal document is one click from copyable.

Her writing sits underneath as a proper archive, because the essays are what make the case that she is worth the fee. The typography does the heavy lifting — a serif that has an opinion, generous measure, nothing that competes with the words.

  • Bio in three lengths — 40, 120 and 300 words — each one copyable
  • Press kit with print-resolution photography and a one-page PDF
  • Talk topics written as outcomes for an audience, not abstracts
  • Essay archive with a citation block on every piece

03

How it was built

Static pages generated from Markdown, so writing an essay means writing a file. The press kit assets are versioned alongside the content, which means the photograph on the site and the photograph in the PDF can never drift apart.

Type is loaded in two weights, subset to Latin, self-hosted. The whole page renders before a webfont request would even resolve.

04

What happened

Speaking enquiries quadrupled in six months, and — Nadia's measure, not mine — the enquiries now arrive already knowing what she talks about, so the first email is about dates rather than an explanation.

I used to spend the first three emails explaining what I do. Now the page has done it and we start at the diary.Dr. Nadia Rahman
