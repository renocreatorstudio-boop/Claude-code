How it works

Four to six weeks, and you always know which week you're in.

No account manager, no relay race, no phase where the project goes quiet. You talk to the person doing the work, every time — because there is only one of us.

Typical duration4–6 weeks Projects at onceOne PriceFixed, before week two Post-launchTwo weeks included

01 — Discovery

A week of questions

Week one

The first week produces no design at all. It produces answers: who actually buys, what stops them, what the last five customers said in the sentence before they signed, and the claim you would never let a stranger write about you.

I read whatever already exists — support inbox, sales calls, analytics, the doc where someone tried to write the positioning eighteen months ago. Most sites fail on a decision made here, not on a decision made in the design.

What you get

  • A written positioning note, one page, that you either agree with or correct
  • The single moment where people currently leave, with the evidence
  • A sitemap and the argument each page is making
  • A fixed price and a dated schedule for the rest

02 — Design

Two directions, not ten

Weeks two and three

You get two real pages, designed in the browser at full size on your own content. Not flat images of a page — the page, on your phone, scrolling. Choosing between two is a decision. Choosing between ten is a lottery, and it is usually a lottery a client is asked to run because nobody wanted to have an opinion.

One round of substantial changes, then one round of refinement. If neither direction is right, that is my problem to solve, not a change request to price.

What you get

  • Two full directions, in the browser, on real content
  • The type scale, colour system and spacing rhythm, documented
  • Every state designed: empty, loading, error, too-long, too-short
  • Mobile designed at the same time, not squeezed afterwards

03 — Build

Written by hand

Weeks three to five

No page builder underneath. That is not craft snobbery — it is the reason the site loads in under a second, works entirely on a keyboard, and reads cleanly to a screen reader on the first pass rather than after a remediation project.

You get a staging link on the first day of build and it stays current. Nothing is revealed at the end. If something is going to feel wrong, I would rather you feel it in week three.

What you get

  • Hand-written HTML, CSS and the minimum JavaScript that does the job
  • A CMS only where you will genuinely edit — never for its own sake
  • Performance budget agreed up front and measured on throttled 4G
  • WCAG 2.2 AA, tested on real assistive technology, not just a linter

04 — Launch

I stay for the landing

Week five, plus two

I run the migration, including the redirects nobody remembers until rankings drop. Then I watch two weeks of real traffic and fix whatever the data argues with — the button nobody presses, the paragraph everybody stops at.

At the end you get the keys, the source, and a short video walking through how to change the things you will actually want to change. Not a 40-page manual neither of us will open again.

What you get

  • Migration, redirects and DNS handled, with a rollback plan
  • Analytics configured to answer questions, not to collect everything
  • Two weeks of post-launch fixes included, not quoted
  • Handover video, source, and no lock-in of any kind

Fair warning

What this is not a good fit for.

A one-person studio is the wrong shape for some work, and it is cheaper for both of us to know that in the first email than in week three.

  • Projects that need five people working in parallel to hit a fixed date
  • Complex product UI with ongoing weekly design — you want an in-house designer
  • Work where the brief is “make it look modern” and there is no decision underneath it
  • Anything requiring me to design by committee against a brand no one will defend

If that is the shape of it, say so and I will point you at someone better suited. That referral is free and I would rather give it than take the job.
