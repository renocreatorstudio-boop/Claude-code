How it works

You always know which week you're in, and what comes next.

Four phases, the same four every time, whether it is a site, an app or a piece of artwork. You talk to the person doing the work at every one of them.

01 — Brief

Questions before pictures

Week one

Before anything gets drawn, I want to know who it is for, what it has to make happen, and the one sentence you would never let a stranger write about you.

Most bad work starts here, with a brief that says “make it look modern” and no decision underneath it. A week of questions is cheaper than five weeks of guessing.

What you get

  • A written note, one page, that you either agree with or correct
  • The single moment where people currently lose interest
  • A map of what is being made and what each piece is arguing
  • A fixed price and a dated schedule for the rest

02 — Direction

Two routes, not ten

Weeks two and three

You get two real directions, on your actual content, at full size — not a grid of thumbnails. Choosing between two is a decision. Choosing between ten is a lottery.

Whatever the medium, mobile is designed at the same time as everything else, never squeezed in afterwards.

What you get

  • Two full directions, on real content, at the size people will see them
  • The type scale, colour system and spacing rhythm, written down
  • Every state designed: empty, loading, error, too long, too short
  • One round of changes on the direction you pick

03 — Make

Built, not assembled

Weeks three to five

Sites and apps are written by hand. No page builder underneath, which is why they load fast, work on a keyboard, and read cleanly to a screen reader on the first pass.

Artwork is made at full resolution and delivered in every format you will actually need, not just the one that looked good in the presentation.

What you get

  • Hand-written HTML, CSS and the minimum JavaScript that does the job
  • A way to edit it yourself, but only where you will genuinely edit
  • A performance budget agreed up front and measured on a slow connection
  • WCAG 2.2 AA, checked properly rather than run through a linter

04 — Hand over

I stay past delivery

Week five, plus two

Launch, migration or final files — whichever the project needs — then two weeks of watching how it behaves in the real world and fixing whatever the data argues with.

Then you get the keys, the source and a short video walking through it. No lock-in of any kind.

What you get

  • Migration, redirects and DNS handled, with a way back if anything breaks
  • Analytics set up to answer questions, not to collect everything
  • Two weeks of post-launch fixes included, not quoted
  • Handover video, source files, and no lock-in

Fair warning

What this is a bad fit for.

  • Work that needs five people running in parallel to hit a fixed date
  • Ongoing weekly design on a product team — you want someone in-house
  • A brief that is “make it look modern” with no decision underneath it
  • Anything designed by committee against a brand nobody will defend
